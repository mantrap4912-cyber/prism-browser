Features

Everything it does, nothing it shouldn’t.

Prism blocks the junk, protects what matters and gets out of your way. Here is what ships in this release.

Ads and trackers, stopped at the door

About 257,000 filter rules from 11 lists block ads, trackers and tracking pixels before they load. Cosmetic rules and scriptlets tidy up whatever is left, and every block is logged with the rule, the list and the layer that caught it.

A calmer YouTube

A built-in guard strips the ad schedule from the page and player data, hides ad slots and skips any ad that still slips through.

New in 0.1.2

Cookie banners, handled

Prism clicks “Reject all” or “Only necessary” for you, even with the ad blocker switched off.

A password vault that keeps its mouth shut

Passwords are sealed with AES-256-GCM using a key derived with PBKDF2 (600,000 rounds). Revealing a password asks for your master password again, and the vault file never contains plain text. A built-in generator makes 24-character passwords.

Private by default

HTTPS-only, DNS-over-HTTPS (Cloudflare by default, or Quad9, Google, Mullvad or your own), tracking parameters stripped, referrers trimmed, third-party cookies blocked and WebRTC leaks held back.

Beta

Bring your own VPN

A built-in WireGuard client plus SOCKS5 and HTTP proxy support, applied to browser traffic only. Prism ships no VPN servers: import your provider’s config.

Tabs your way

A vertical tab rail or classic top tabs, with workspaces, containers and split view for keeping life and work apart.

Keyboard first

Press Ctrl+K for the command palette: jump to settings, history, a tab or an action without touching the mouse.

New in 0.1.2

Make every new tab yours

Pick a built-in background or use your own photo, then fine-tune fit, dim and blur. Choose light or dark, an accent colour, compact density and a Windows 11 Mica frame, or turn animations down entirely.

Reader, find, full screen

Reader view for long articles, find in page, F11 full screen, and your tabs restored when you come back.

Light on your PC

Starts in about two seconds. Internal pages such as settings and history share one UI process, and a live memory readout sits in the title bar so you always know what Prism costs.

Private windows

Ctrl+Shift+N opens a private window for the browsing you don’t want kept, with the same shield and the same shortcuts.

How the shield works

Three layers between you and the noise.

Blocking happens before pages load, while they load, and after they render. That is why it catches what simple extensions miss.

Every request, inspected

Traffic router

Every request passes through a small local router first. Hosts on the blocklist are refused before a connection is even made.

02

Filter engine

Page and frame requests are matched against 11 compiled lists. Each decision names the rule, the list and the layer, so you can see why something was blocked.

03

Page guard

Cosmetic rules hide empty ad slots, scriptlets neutralise tracking scripts, and site-specific guards (like YouTube’s) clean up what only shows up at runtime.

Privacy

Built so you don’t have to take our word for it.

Prism keeps your browsing on your computer and shows its work: you can see what was blocked and why.

Your data lives on your PC

History, bookmarks, the vault and settings are stored in a PrismData folder on your computer (on drive D: if you have one, otherwise next to the program).

No analytics inside Prism

There is no analytics or tracking SDK in the app. Its only background traffic is refreshing the filter lists, checking once a day whether a newer version exists (one small public file, no ID; both can be switched off in Settings) and DNS-over-HTTPS to the provider you pick.

Controls that do what they say

If a setting can’t work yet, it is removed rather than faked. An unreachable VPN server reports a failure instead of showing a made-up IP.

Uninstalls cleanly

Remove Prism from Windows Settings → Apps. The program goes; your data folder stays until you delete it.

FAQ

Questions, answered honestly.

Is Prism Browser free?

Yes. Prism Browser is free to download and use.

Windows says “Windows protected your PC”. Is the installer safe?

Prism is a new app from an independent publisher and the installer isn’t code-signed yet, so Windows SmartScreen shows a warning for any file it hasn’t seen many times. Choose “More info” → “Run anyway”. You can confirm the file is exactly the one we published by comparing its SHA-256 hash with the one on the download page.

Which versions of Windows are supported?

Windows 10 (version 2004 or newer) and Windows 11, 64-bit. Prism renders pages with the Microsoft Edge WebView2 Runtime, which ships with Windows 11 and is installed on most Windows 10 PCs.

Does Prism collect my data?

Prism contains no analytics or tracking code. It keeps your data on your PC. In the background it refreshes its filter lists, checks for a newer version by downloading one small public file from GitHub (once at start and then daily, with no ID and nothing about you; you can turn both off in Settings) and, when DNS-over-HTTPS is on, sends DNS queries to the provider you chose.

Will blocking break the sites I use?

Occasionally. If a page misbehaves, open the shield button in the address bar and switch protection off for that site. It is remembered for next time.

Does the built-in VPN work?

Prism includes a WireGuard client and SOCKS5/HTTP proxy support for browser traffic. It does not include any VPN servers: you import the config from your own provider. It is marked Beta because it is still being tested against more providers.

Is there an Android or Mac version?

Windows only for now. An Android version is in development. There is no macOS or Linux build at the moment.

How do I uninstall Prism?

Open Windows Settings → Apps → Installed apps, find Prism Browser and choose Uninstall. Your PrismData folder is left in place so you never lose bookmarks by accident.
